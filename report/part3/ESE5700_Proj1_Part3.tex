\subsection{Architecture}\label{sec:arch}
Two copies of the Part 2 macro hold the weights $W$ and activations $X$; a static-CMOS array multiplier, a 12-bit ripple-carry accumulator, a 4-bit address counter and a two-bit controller (all registers are transmission-gate master-slave flip-flops) compute $\mathrm{ACC}=\sum_{i=0}^{15}W[i]\,X[i]$. The read is pipelined against the multiply-accumulate (read in cycle $n$, accumulate in $n+1$), so a dot product takes 17 cycles from START to DONE. Tests A, B and C all pass at $10$, $4.0$ and $2.5$ ns. At $2.0$ ns, A finishes at 0x34D and C at 0x320 (pass) while B finishes at 0xC10 instead of 0xE10 (fail). At $1.75$ ns and $1.5$ ns all three fail. The selected period is therefore $T_{CLK}=2.5$ ns ($400$ MHz), $t_{dot}=42.5$ ns; the $2.0$--$2.5$ ns interval was not swept further. Energy and FOM use that same period: Test A $8.870$ pJ, FOM$_{MAC}=1.1\times10^{5}\ \mu$m$^2\cdot$pJ$\cdot$ns. The multiplier's worst case is about $1.2$ ns, so it, not the $488$ ps SRAM read, sets the period. Area $291.8\ \mu$m$^2$ \est.

\section{Sequential elements}\label{sec:seq}

\subsection{Style and justification}
Positive-edge master-slave flip-flop built from two static transmission-gate latches (\code{dlatch}): the master is transparent while CLK $=0$, the slave while CLK $=1$, so $D$ is captured on the rising edge. Each latch has an input \code{tg}, a storage node driven by two \code{inv\_2x}, a hold \code{tg} that closes the loop from $Q$, and its own clock inverter; the flip-flop adds one more \code{inv\_2x} to make the master's clock (100 fins: \code{tg} 2$+$2 fins, \code{inv\_2x} 8/4 fins). It was chosen because it reuses the verified \code{tg} and inverter cells, is fully static (holds with the clock stopped, unlike a dynamic C$^2$MOS latch), and needs only the single external clock. Its cost is the local clock inverters (clock load, and a small skew between the two phases, which hold must cover). \code{register\_1b} adds a \code{mux2} load enable ($EN=1$ loads $D$, $EN=0$ recirculates $Q$) and an \code{and2} synchronous reset (152 fins). A C$^2$MOS or pulsed latch would cut the local clock load ($C_{CLK}=2.822$ fF) and some of the $68.51$ ps $t_{cq}$, but cannot hold with the clock stopped, which the sequencer does in IDLE and DONE.

\textbf{Design note.} The first \code{dlatch} fed the node after one inversion back through the hold gate, an inverting loop that cannot store a value; the hold \code{tg} now returns $Q$ (two inversions) to the storage node and conducts while CLK $=0$ (fixed 10/25, before any register simulation).

\subsection{Energy per clock}
$V_{DD}=0.8$ V, $C_Q=1$ fF, 20 clocks from $10$ ns to $90$ ns. Energy drawn from the VDD source (external CLK-driver energy excluded):
\[
E=\frac{-V_{DD}}{20}\int_{10\,\mathrm{ns}}^{90\,\mathrm{ns}}I_{V0+}\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]
Idle (\code{dff\_energy\_idle.vec}, D constant): $3.895$ fJ/clock. Toggling (\code{dff\_energy\_toggle.vec}): $9.309$ fJ/clock. Extra energy due to data switching: $9.309-3.895=5.414$ fJ/clock.

\subsection{Clock-input capacitance}
On \code{tb\_dff\_cclk}, CLK is driven by \code{vpulse} V1 ($0\to0.8$ V, $t_r=20$ ps); VDD is $0.8$ V; $C_Q=1$ fF. Effective rising-edge capacitance from the charge delivered by the CLK source:
\[

\subsection{Multiplier}
Array multiplier: 16 AND gates form the partial products $a_ib_j$ (four \code{and2x4}, one per row $j$), and three 4-bit ripple-carry adders (\code{fulladder\_4b}, 12 full adders) sum the rows, each row shifted by one bit; $P_0=a_0b_0$, $P_{1..3}$ from the low bit of each adder row, $P_{4..7}$ from the last row and its carry. It reuses one verified full-adder cell in a regular array, which made it quick to build and debug; the cost is speed, because the longest path ripples through all three adder rows into the last row's carry ($P_6$, $P_7$). A carry-save (Wallace or Dadda) tree would shorten the path to two full-adder levels plus one final carry-propagate adder, for the same 12 full adders but irregular wiring. 2096 fins.
\fig{mult4x4_schematic}{.98}{\code{multiplier4b} (corrected): partial-product rows I4, I6, I8, I10 (\code{and2x4}, row $j$ gets $A\langle0..3\rangle$ and $B_j$ on all four B pins) and ripple rows I5, I7, I9 (\code{fulladder\_4b}, $C_{in}=0$); each row adds the previous row's upper sum bits and carry to the next partial product. Sizes ($L=18$ nm): \code{and2} $=$ \code{nand2\_u} (PMOS 2$\parallel$2, NMOS 2$+$2 series fins) $+$ \code{inv\_2x} (8/4); full adder: two \code{xor2} (NMOS 2/4, PMOS 2 fins, input \code{inv\_2x}), two \code{and2}, one \code{or2}.}

\subsection{Logical-effort analysis}\label{sec:le}
Delay of each stage in units of $\tau=10.5$ ps (Part 2: FO4 $=53$ ps $=5\tau$, chain of \code{inv\_u}) as $d=f+p$, with the effort $f=g\,h$ computed directly from fin counts: $f=R\cdot C_{\text{load}}/C_{\text{inv\_u}}$, where $R$ is the gate's worst-edge pull-up or pull-down resistance relative to \code{inv\_u} (PMOS 4 / NMOS 2 fins, $C_{\text{inv\_u}}=6$ fins) and $C_{\text{load}}$ the gate fins it drives. A full-adder input or carry-in (one \code{xor2} input: \code{inv\_2x} 12 fins $+$ two 2-fin devices; plus one \code{nand2\_u} input, 4 fins) is 20 fins.
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L l r r r r@{}}\toprule Stage & Load & $R$ (worst) & $f$ & $p$ & $d$ [$\tau$]\\\midrule
\code{inv\_2x} (xor2 input inverter) & 4 fins & 0.5 & 0.3 & 1 & 1.3\\\addlinespace
\code{xor2} core (2-series: PMOS $2{+}2$, NMOS $2{+}4$ fins) & 20 fins & 2.75 & 9.2 & 3 & 12.2\\\addlinespace
\code{nand2\_u} ($2{+}2$ series, $2\parallel2$) & 12 fins & 2 & 4 & 2 & 6\\\addlinespace
\code{inv\_2x} (and2 output) & 4 fins & 0.5 & 0.3 & 1 & 1.3\\\addlinespace
\code{nor2} in \code{or2} (PMOS $2{+}2$ series) & 12 fins & 2.5 & 5 & 2 & 7\\\addlinespace
\code{inv\_2x} (or2 output $=C_{out}$) & 20 fins & 0.5 & 1.7 & 1 & 2.7\\\bottomrule\end{tabularx}\end{center}
Full-adder arcs: $C_{in}\to C_{out}$ (and2 $+$ or2) $=6+1.3+7+2.7\approx17\tau$; $C_{in}\to S$ (one xor2) $\approx13\tau$; $A/B\to S$ (two xor2) $\approx26\tau$; $A/B\to C_{out}$ (xor2, and2, or2) $\approx30\tau$; a partial-product \code{and2} into a full-adder input $\approx6+2.7\approx9\tau$.

\textbf{Multiplier critical path.} With ripple rows, the longest path alternates carry and sum through all three rows and then ripples along the last one: $a_ib_j$ (and2) $\to$ I5 bit 0 ($A/B\to C_{out}$) $\to$ I5 bit 1 ($C_{in}\to S$) $\to$ I7 bit 0 ($A/B\to C_{out}$) $\to$ I7 bit 1 ($C_{in}\to S$) $\to$ I9 bit 0 ($A/B\to C_{out}$) $\to$ I9 bits 1--3 ($C_{in}\to C_{out}$) $\to P_7$:
\[ D_{\text{mult}}\approx 9+30+13+30+13+30+3\times17=176\,\tau\approx1.85\text{ ns}. \]
Measured worst case: 1.14--1.24 ns. The estimate is about 50\% high, as expected: it charges every stage its slower edge and the full 20-fin load, and the slowest simulated vector (244) need not excite the whole alternating path. Both agree on what dominates: the \code{xor2} core, whose 2-fin series PMOS stack makes it about 4$\times$ weaker than \code{inv\_u} on the rising edge (12 of the 26 $\tau$ in a sum arc). \textbf{Sizing from this analysis:} widening the \code{xor2} stacks to 4-fin PMOS and NMOS ($R\approx1$) cuts each xor2 stage from $\approx12\tau$ to $\approx5\tau$ and the multiplier path to $\approx120\tau\approx1.3$ ns (LE); the cells were simulated at the drawn sizes.

\textbf{Accumulator adder.} The product bits arrive at different times; the late ones are the upper bits. Worst case: $P_4$ settles last, then $A/B\to C_{out}$ at bit 4, carries through bits 5--10 and $C_{in}\to S$ at bit 11:
\[
D_{\mathrm{add}}=30+6\times17+13=145\,\tau\approx1.5\,\mathrm{ns}.
\]
A carry from ACC bit 0 through all 12 bits,
\[
30+11\times17\approx217\,\tau\approx2.3\,\mathrm{ns},
\]
starts at $t_{cq}$ after the edge and so ends earlier than the path through the multiplier. The register input adds the \code{mux2} and \code{and2} before the flip-flop, $\approx10\tau\approx0.1$ ns.

Encoding (from \code{controller\_mac}): two state registers, $S_0$ and DONE; IDLE $=(S_0,\mathrm{DONE})=(0,0)$, COMPUTE: RUN $=S_0\overline{\mathrm{DONE}}=1$, DONE $=(0,1)$. Two more registers carry the pipeline: VALID ($=$ RUN one cycle late, so the first product is accumulated one cycle after its read) and LAST\_READ (set the cycle after the counter reaches 15). RST clears $S_0$ and DONE from any state. Per state:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{IDLE / DONE, START high:} $G_0=\mathrm{START}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{RUN}}$ raises CLEAR (clears the accumulator, the counter, VALID and LAST\_READ) and sets $S_0$; DONE clears. START is ignored while RUN.
\item \textbf{COMPUTE:} COUNT\_EN $=\mathrm{RUN}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{LAST}}$ steps the counter 0 to 15 and holds it at 15 (LAST $=$ count $=15$); ACC\_EN $=\mathrm{RUN}\cdot\mathrm{VALID}$; $S_0$ stays set while LAST\_READ $=0$.
\item \textbf{Leaving COMPUTE:} FINISH $=\mathrm{RUN}\cdot\mathrm{LAST\_READ}$ sets DONE and clears $S_0$, so the exit is one cycle after the count reaches 15, which leaves time to accumulate the last product.
\item \textbf{DONE:} held by $\mathrm{DONE}\cdot\overline{\mathrm{START}}$; ACC holds because ACC\_EN $=0$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Timing closure}\label{sec:timing}

\subsection{Critical path}
The pipeline has no register between the macros and the multiplier: the macro's own address latch captures COUNT at the rising edge and its output latch holds DOUT for the rest of the cycle. The paths that must fit in one period are therefore (1) counter $t_{cq}$ $\to$ \code{mux2} $\to$ macro address latch (short), and (2) CLK edge $\to$ SRAM access $\to$ multiplier $\to$ 12-bit adder $\to$ accumulator setup, the critical path: $T_{\min}\ge t_{\text{SRAM}}+t_{\text{mult}}+t_{\text{add}}+t_{su}$, with $t_{\text{SRAM}}$ measured from CLK and so already including the macro's internal address latch.
\begin{align*}
T_{\min,\text{non-pipe}}
  &\ge t_{cq}+t_{\text{SRAM}}+t_{\text{mult}}+t_{\text{add}}+t_{su}+t_{\text{skew}},\\
T_{\min,\text{pipe}}
  &\ge \max\bigl(t_{cq}+t_{\text{SRAM}}+t_{su},\;
                 t_{cq}+t_{\text{mult}}+t_{\text{add}}+t_{su}\bigr).
\end{align*}

\subsection{Hold check}
Two short paths: (1) counter $\to$ \code{mux2} $\to$ macro address latch, which closes at the same rising edge that changes the counter; (2) register-to-register in the controller (VALID from RUN through one \code{and2}). From \sref{sec:hold}, $t_{cq,\min}=62.18$ ps and $t_{h}=10$ ps (rise; fall $\le0$ ps), so $t_{cq,\min}-t_h=52$ ps of hold slack before mux/logic delay. No hold fail was seen on \code{tb\_top} at $2.5$ ns or $10$ ns.

\subsection{Read-latch fix}
The first runs of \code{tb\_top} read ACC $=0$ although the sequencer was correct (DONE 17 edges after START). Probing inside \code{top}: WE, ADDR and DIN reach both macros correctly, the wordlines fire and the read inverter output RD rises after each read, but both DOUT buses stay at 0. The macro's output latch was enabled by a pulse derived from the delayed wordline enable (RD\_EN $=$ WLD$\cdot\overline{\mathrm{WE\_L}}$, about 150 ps wide), which closed before RD had crossed mid-rail, so the latch node peaked near 0.4 V and fell back. RD\_EN is now level-sensitive on the clock-high phase (RD\_EN $=$ CLKBUF$\cdot\overline{\mathrm{WE\_L}}$, one input of \code{I11} in \code{controller}): the latch follows RD for the whole high phase and closes about 40 ps before precharge pulls RD down after the falling edge. The Part 2 \code{tb\_sram\_macro} was not re-run with that RD\_EN change; \code{tb\_top} Tests A--C after the fix are the check.

\subsection{Energy}
From the single VDD source on \code{tb\_top} at $T=2.5$ ns (vectors \code{mac\_top\_A\_2p5ns.vec}, \code{mac\_top\_B\_2p5ns.vec}, \code{mac\_top\_C\_2p5ns.vec}). External vector-driver and CLK-source energy are excluded. The sign convention is $-V_{DD}\int I_{V0+}\,\mathrm{d}t$: Spectre's device current $I_{V0+}$ leaves the positive terminal of V0, so the chip energy is the negative of that integral.
\begin{align*}
E_{dot}
&= -V_{DD}\int_{82\,\mathrm{ns}}^{126\,\mathrm{ns}} I_{V0+}\,\mathrm{d}t
= -0.8\int_{82\,\mathrm{ns}}^{126\,\mathrm{ns}} I(\texttt{/V0/PLUS})\,\mathrm{d}t.
\end{align*}
ViVA calculator (result in pJ):
\begin{center}
\code{-0.8 * value(integ(clip(IT("/V0/PLUS") 82n 126n)) 126n) * 1e12}
\end{center}
The clip $82$--$126$ ns is $44$ ns. That is the recorded Cadence window, not the latency:
\[
t_{dot}=N_{cycles}\,T_{CLK}=17\times2.5\,\mathrm{ns}=42.5\,\mathrm{ns}.
\]

\subsection{Memory vs.\ datapath energy}\label{sec:memenergy}
\code{tb\_top} has one VDD pin (V0) feeding both SRAM macros and the MAC logic, and this environment has no Cadence session in which to probe hierarchical instance currents, so the split below is an estimate from the Part 2 macro measurements, not a second Spectre integral. It is labeled \est\ throughout.

Part 2 measured the macro's own VDD at $T_{CLK}=0.69$ ns: checkerboard read $P_{\mathrm{read}}=115.2\ \mu$W, clock-stopped leakage $P_{\mathrm{leak}}=3.99\ \mu$W, clock-running idle $112.2\ \mu$W. Dynamic energy per access is then
\[
E_{\mathrm{dyn}}=(P_{\mathrm{read}}-P_{\mathrm{leak}})\,T_{CLK,\mathrm{SRAM}}
=(115.2-3.99)\,\mu\mathrm{W}\times0.69\,\mathrm{ns}=76.7\,\mathrm{fJ}.
\]
At the MAC's $2.5$ ns period the same access still costs about $E_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, plus leakage over the longer cycle:
\begin{align*}
E_{\mathrm{leak}}
&=P_{\mathrm{leak}}\times2.5\,\mathrm{ns}
=3.99\,\mu\mathrm{W}\times2.5\,\mathrm{ns}
=10.0\,\mathrm{fJ},\\
E_{\mathrm{macro/cyc}}
&\approx76.7+10.0
=86.7\,\mathrm{fJ}.
\end{align*}
Both macros are read in each of the 16 compute addresses:
\[
E_{\mathrm{mem}}\approx 2\times16\times86.7\,\mathrm{fJ}=2.77\,\mathrm{pJ}\ \est.
\]
Bitline restore alone is much smaller:
\[
E_{BL}
=2\times16\times4\times C_{BL}V_{DD}^{2}
=2\times16\times4\times3.5\,\mathrm{fF}\times(0.8\,\mathrm{V})^{2}
=0.29\,\mathrm{pJ}.
\]
The rest of $E_{\mathrm{mem}}$ is the Part 2 delay-chain controller, precharge and output latches, which fire on every clock (no chip-select). That matches Part 2: clock-running idle ($112\ \mu$W) is almost the read power ($115\ \mu$W), so SRAM energy is nearly pattern-independent.

\]

\subsection{Delay}
The period is set by the multiply-accumulate path, not the memory. The array multiplier's worst case is about $1.2$ ns---over twice the $488$ ps SRAM read---before the 12-bit ripple adder and $t_{su}=40$ ps. Both arithmetic blocks are ripple structures from one small full adder (2-fin \code{xor2} stacks with input inverters): easy to verify, long in delay.

\subsection{Area and energy}
The datapath ($138\ \mu$m$^2$) is larger than the two macros ($109\ \mu$m$^2$ \est), mostly the 24 full adders and the 12 accumulator registers. SRAM energy is dominated by the clocked Part~2 controller (no chip-select), not by bitline swing, so it barely moves from A to B; the $6.4$ pJ extra on Test~B is the full-scale carry path.

\subsection{Choices we kept}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.55em}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{0.4em}
\item \textbf{Register.} Static TG master-slave, 100 fins per flip-flop (152 with enable and reset), reused everywhere. A dynamic latch would be smaller and faster but cannot hold with the clock stopped (IDLE and DONE do). Measured $t_{cq}=68.51/62.18$ ps, $t_{su}=40$ ps, $t_{h}=10/\le0$ ps, $C_{CLK}=2.822$ fF, $E=3.895$ fJ idle / $9.309$ fJ toggling.
\item \textbf{Pipeline.} Overlapping the read with the multiply-accumulate costs one cycle (17 instead of 16) and stays within the 20-cycle limit. The macro output latch is the only pipeline stage, so SRAM access and arithmetic still share one period. A register after the multiplier would split them, at a second extra cycle (18).
\item \textbf{Arithmetic.} Ripple array and ripple adder: smallest design effort and regular wiring, but the slowest choice. We kept them because they reused one verified full adder and left the macros as the Part~2 black box.
\end{itemize}

A carry-save multiplier, a faster final adder, or a pipeline register after the multiplier would cut the period. A chip-select on the macros would cut the clocked memory energy.
