latexmath:[\beta = (W/L)_{\mathrm{PD}} / (W/L)_{\mathrm{PG}}]

pull-up ratio stem:[\gamma] guards the write (pull-up vs. pass-gate).

[#bitcell-read-stability]
=== Cell Ratio and Read Stability

// Source section: hand notes §1.1.1.

For the 6T SRAM, assume stem:[q = 0] for read-stability analysis.
Given stem:[q = 0], the node is pulled down by the NMOS inside inverter 1.

.6T SRAM cell used for read-stability analysis
image::6t-cell-wl-bl-inv12-q-qb.png[6T SRAM cell used for read-stability analysis,align=center,width=640]

The read path can first be approximated using a voltage-divider model.

.Approximate voltage-divider model
image::voltage_div_diagram.png[Approximate voltage-divider model,align=center,width=640]

With stem:[BL = V_{DD}], the pass-gate resistance is stem:[R_{PG}] and
the pull-down resistance is stem:[R_{PD1}].

By voltage division,

[stem]
++++
\frac{V_q}{V_{DD}}
=
\frac{R_{PD1}}{R_{PG} + R_{PD1}}
++++

or equivalently,

[stem]
++++
\frac{V_q}{V_{DD}}
=
\frac{1}
{1 + \frac{R_{PG}}{R_{PD1}}}
++++

From transistor physics,

[stem]
++++
R_T \propto \frac{1}{W_T}
++++

Assume that the pass transistor and pull-down transistor have the same
channel length.

Define the cell ratio

[stem]
++++
\beta
=
\frac{(W/L)_{PD}}{(W/L)_{PG}}
=
\frac{W_{PD}}{W_{PG}}
++++

Therefore,

[stem]
++++
\beta
\approx
\frac{R_{PG}}{R_{PD}}
++++

and hence the voltage-divider approximation becomes

[stem]
++++
\frac{V_q}{V_{DD}}
\approx
\frac{1}{1+\beta}
++++

so that

[stem]
++++
V_q
\approx
\frac{V_{DD}}{1+\beta}.
++++

From this approximation, increasing the selected value of stem:[\beta]
reduces the value of stem:[V_q].

The relevance of this result is reinforced by considering the transistor
transition voltage. Let stem:[V_m] denote the inverter switching point,
where both the PMOS and NMOS are conducting and

[stem]
++++
V_{in} = V_{out} = V_m.
++++

At stem:[V_m], the inverter is at its transition point and is not stable
with respect to a prior logic-state voltage.


[#bitcell-current-balance]
=== MOSFET Current-Balance Analysis

// Source section: hand notes §1.1.2.

At the equilibrium read-disturb voltage, the storage-node voltage is
constant.

Neglecting gate leakage and other secondary currents, Kirchhoff's current
law implies that the pull-down and pass-gate currents are equal:

[stem]
++++
I_{pd} = I_{pg}
++++

or, in terms of current magnitudes,

[stem]
++++
|I_{pg}| = |I_{pd}|.
++++

// TODO(figures): crop the current-balance sketch (BL — R_PG — I_pg —> q,
// R_PD1 — I_pd —> GND, hand notes §1.1.2) to
// media/part1/current-balance.png and reference it here.
// No placeholder is rendered — nothing fabricated.

==== Pass-Gate NMOS

For the pass transistor,

[stem]
++++
D = V_{DD},
\qquad
G = V_{DD},
\qquad
S = V_q.
++++

Therefore,

[stem]
++++
V_{DS} = V_{DD} - V_q
++++

and

[stem]
++++
V_{GS} = V_{DD} - V_q.
++++

Since

[stem]
++++
V_{DS} = V_{GS},
++++

the saturation condition

[stem]
++++
V_{DS} > V_{GS} - V_{tn}
++++

is satisfied, so the pass transistor operates in saturation.

Its current is therefore

[stem]
++++
I_{pg}
=
\frac{1}{2}
k_{pg}
\left(V_{GS}-V_{tn}\right)^2
++++

with

[stem]
++++
k_{pg}
=
\mu_n C_{ox}
\left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_{pg}.
++++

.Pass-gate saturation current and transconductance factor — handwritten source
image::ip_kpg_cox_vgs_vth_w_l_eq.png[Handwritten pass-gate current equations,align=center,width=640]


==== Pull-Down NMOS

For the pull-down NMOS, stem:[pd1],

[stem]
++++
V_D = V_q,
\qquad
V_G = \overline{V_q} \approx V_{DD},
\qquad
V_S = 0.
++++

Therefore,

[stem]
++++
V_{DS} = V_q
++++

and

[stem]
++++
V_{GS} = V_{DD}.
++++

For the read-disturb condition,

[stem]
++++
V_{DS} < V_{GS} - V_{tn},
++++

so the pull-down transistor operates in the triode region.

The pull-down current is therefore

[stem]
++++
I_{pd}
=
k_{pd}
\left[
\left(V_{GS}-V_{tn}\right)V_{DS}
-
\frac{V_{DS}^2}{2}
\right]
++++

where

[stem]
++++
k_{pd}
=
\mu_n C_{ox}
\left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_{PD}.
++++

At the equilibrium read-disturb voltage,

[stem]
++++
I_{pd} = I_{pg}.
++++


==== Solving for the Read-Disturb Voltage

Substituting the device voltages into the current expressions gives

[stem]
++++
k_{pd}
\left[
\left(V_{DD}-V_{tn}\right)V_q
-
\frac{V_q^2}{2}
\right]
=
\frac{1}{2}
k_{pg}
\left(V_{DD}-V_q-V_{tn}\right)^2.
++++

Rearranging,

[stem]
++++
\frac{k_{pd}}{k_{pg}}
=
\frac
{\left(V_{DD}-V_q-V_{tn}\right)^2}
{2\left(V_{DD}-V_{tn}\right)V_q - V_q^2}.
++++

Let

[stem]
++++
E = V_{DD}-V_{tn}.
++++

Then

[stem]
++++
\frac{k_{pd}}{k_{pg}}
=
\frac{(E-V_q)^2}
{2EV_q-V_q^2}.
++++

.Strength ratio in terms of the disturb voltage — handwritten source
image::kpd_kpg_e_vq_evq_eq.png[Handwritten k_pd over k_pg equation,align=center,width=640]

Also,

[stem]
++++
\frac{k_{pd}}{k_{pg}}
=
\frac{(W/L)_{PD}}{(W/L)_{PG}}
=
\beta.
++++

Thus,

[stem]
++++
\left(2EV_q-V_q^2\right)\beta
=
(E-V_q)^2.
++++

Expanding,

[stem]
++++
2E\beta V_q-\beta V_q^2
=
E^2+V_q^2-2EV_q.
++++

Collecting terms,

[stem]
++++
V_q^2(1+\beta)
+
V_q(-2E-2E\beta)
+
E^2
=
0.
++++

Using the quadratic formula,

[stem]
++++
V_q
=
E
\left(
1
\pm
\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}
\right).
++++

The positive solution exceeds stem:[V_{DD}-V_{tn}] and is inconsistent
with the assumed conducting pass gate and triode-region pull-down
transistor.

Therefore the physically relevant solution is

[stem]
++++
\boxed{
V_q
=
(V_{DD}-V_{tn})
\left(
1-
\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}
\right)
}.
++++

.Read-disturb voltage in closed form — handwritten source
image::vq_vdd_vth_beta_eq.png[Handwritten V_q closed-form equation,align=center,width=640]

This result illustrates that increasing the cell ratio stem:[\beta]
reduces the voltage developed at the stored-zero node during a read.

The derivation assumes:

* ideal long-channel square-law behavior,
* equal NMOS threshold voltages,
* negligible body effect, and
* stem:[\overline{Q} \approx V_{DD}].

See <<bitcell-write-stability,Pull-up Ratio and Write Stability>> for the
writability / stem:[\gamma] analysis.


[#bitcell-switching]
=== Inverter Switching Voltage

// Source section: hand notes §1.1.3.

The disturbed storage node stem:[q] is connected to the input of
inverter 2.

// The SRAM-cell figure is reused here because it shows q driving INV2.
.6T SRAM cell showing the disturbed node connected to inverter 2
image::6t-cell-wl-bl-inv12-q-qb.png[6T SRAM cell showing q connected to inverter 2,align=center,width=640]

The switching voltage is defined by

[stem]
++++
V_{in} = V_{out} = V_m.
++++

At the inverter's DC switching point, the NMOS and PMOS drain-current
magnitudes are equal:

[stem]
++++
I_{DN} = |I_{DP}|.
++++

Assuming both MOSFETs are in saturation and using square-law current
equations,

[stem]
++++
\frac{1}{2}
k_n
(V_m-V_{tn})^2
=
\frac{1}{2}
k_p
(V_{DD}-V_m-|V_{tp}|)^2.
++++

Therefore,

[stem]
++++
\frac{k_p}{k_n}
=
\frac{(V_m-V_{tn})^2}
{(V_{DD}-V_m-|V_{tp}|)^2}.
++++

Define

[stem]
++++
B
=
\sqrt{\frac{k_p}{k_n}}.
++++

Then

[stem]
++++
B
\left(
V_{DD}-V_m-|V_{tp}|
\right)
=
V_m-V_{tn}.
++++

Solving for stem:[V_m],

[stem]
++++
V_m
=
\frac
{B(V_{DD}-|V_{tp}|)+V_{tn}}
{1+B}.
++++

Equivalently,

[stem]
++++
\boxed{
V_m
=
\frac
{V_{tn}+B(V_{DD}-|V_{tp}|)}
{1+B}
}.
++++


// NOTE:
// The following PG / gamma notation is preserved as written in the
// handwritten source.

Also,

[stem]
++++
\frac{k_p}{k_n}
=
\frac
{\mu_p C_{ox}(W/L)_{PU}}
{\mu_n C_{ox}(W/L)_{PG}}.
++++

Define

[stem]
++++
\beta = (W/L)_{PD},
\qquad
\gamma = (W/L)_{PG}.
++++

The handwritten derivation then writes

[stem]
++++
\frac{k_p}{k_n}
=
\frac{\gamma \mu_p}
{\beta \mu_n}
=
B^2.
++++

Therefore,

[stem]
++++
B
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\mu_p \gamma}
{\mu_n \beta}
}.
++++

Substituting this into the switching-voltage equation gives

[stem]
++++
\boxed{
V_m
=
\frac{
V_{tn}
+
\sqrt{
\frac{\mu_p\gamma}
{\mu_n\beta}
}
\left(V_{DD}-|V_{tp}|\right)
}{
1+
\sqrt{
\frac{\mu_p\gamma}
{\mu_n\beta}
}
}
}.
++++

Thus, the inverter switching voltage depends on the supply voltage,
transistor threshold voltages, and the relative PMOS/NMOS strengths.


[#bitcell-selection]
=== Read-Stability Condition and Cell-Ratio Selection

// Source section: hand notes §1.1.4.

For read stability, require the disturbed stored-zero voltage to remain
below the inverter switching voltage:

[stem]
++++
V_q < V_m.
++++

Using the expressions derived above,

[stem]
++++
(V_{DD}-V_{tn})
\left(
1-
\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}
\right)
<
\frac{
V_{tn}
+
\sqrt{
\frac{\mu_p\gamma}
{\mu_n\beta}
}
\left(V_{DD}-|V_{tp}|\right)
}{
1+
\sqrt{
\frac{\mu_p\gamma}
{\mu_n\beta}
}
}.
++++

Increasing stem:[\beta] strengthens the pull-down transistor relative to
the pass gate and reduces the read-disturb voltage.

However, stem:[\beta] also affects the inverter switching voltage and,
importantly, the cell area.

Therefore, the final cell ratio cannot be selected simply by minimizing
stem:[V_q].

[#bitcell-write-stability]
=== Pull-up Ratio and Write Stability

// Source: ESE5700_Proj1_Part1.tex §1 "Write stability"
// (Eqs eq:w-divider, eq:w-divider-gamma, eq:w-quadratic, eq:w-vq,
// eq:w-condition, eq:w-gamma; Tabs tab:w-gamma, tab:w-strength;
// Fig fig:w-divider TikZ).

Assume the stored state stem:[Q = 1] (stem:[\overline{Q} \approx 0])
for the write analysis, with a 0 written through BL. With BL driven to
stem:[0\,\mathrm{V}] and WL at stem:[V_{DD}], the pass-gate pulls the
“1” node down while the PMOS inside INV1, still on because
stem:[\overline{Q} \approx 0], pulls it up to stem:[V_{DD}].

==== Write voltage-divider model

The written node therefore follows an approximate voltage-divider model:

[stem]
++++
\frac{V_q}{V_{DD}}
=
\frac{R_{PG}}{R_{PG} + R_{PU1}}
=
\frac{1}{1 + R_{PU1}/R_{PG}}
++++

From transistor physics stem:[R_T \propto 1/(\mu_T W_T)] at fixed
stem:[L]. The two devices are of opposite type, so the mobility ratio
does not cancel as it does in the read divider. Defining the pull-up
ratio

[stem]
++++
\gamma
=
\frac{(W/L)_{PU}}{(W/L)_{PG}}
=
\frac{W_{PU}}{W_{PG}}
++++

gives stem:[R_{PU1}/R_{PG} \approx \mu_n/(\mu_p\gamma)], hence

[stem]
++++
\frac{V_q}{V_{DD}}
\approx
\frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{\mu_n}{\mu_p\gamma}},
\qquad
V_q
\approx
V_{DD}\,\frac{\mu_p\gamma}{\mu_n + \mu_p\gamma}.
++++

A smaller stem:[\gamma] lowers the written node. Whether it falls far
enough to flip the cell is set by the switching voltage stem:[V_m]
(stem:[V_{in} = V_{out}]) of INV2, which stem:[Q] drives — the
stem:[V_m] expression derived in <<bitcell-switching,Inverter Switching
Voltage>>.

NOTE: The LaTeX report draws this divider as a TikZ figure
(Fig `fig:w-divider`: supply through stem:[R_{PU1}] against
stem:[R_{PG}] to the 0 V bitline). No PNG is staged under
`media/part1/` for it, so no `image::` is rendered here — nothing
fabricated.

==== MOSFET current balance at the write point

Neglecting gate leakage, KCL gives
stem:[I_{PU} = I_{PG}] at the equilibrium write voltage.

Pass-gate: stem:[G = V_{DD}], stem:[S = \mathrm{BL} = 0] and
stem:[D = Q], so stem:[V_{GS} = V_{DD}] and
stem:[V_{DS} = V_q < V_{GS} - V_{tn}] at the equilibrium point:
triode, with

[stem]
++++
I_{PG}
=
k_{PG}\left[(V_{DD} - V_{tn})V_q - V_q^2/2\right]
++++

and stem:[k_{PG} = \mu_n C_{ox}(W/L)_{PG}].

Pull-up (pu1): stem:[S = V_{DD}], stem:[G = \overline{Q} \approx 0]
and stem:[D = Q], so stem:[V_{SG} = V_{DD}] and
stem:[V_{SD} = V_{DD} - V_q > V_{SG} - |V_{tp}|] as long as
stem:[V_q < |V_{tp}|]: saturation, with

[stem]
++++
I_{PU}
=
\tfrac{1}{2}k_{PU}(V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|)^2
++++

and stem:[k_{PU} = \mu_p C_{ox}(W/L)_{PU}].

Equating the two with stem:[E = V_{DD} - V_{tn}],
stem:[F = V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|] and
stem:[k_{PU}/k_{PG} = \gamma\,\mu_p/\mu_n] yields

[stem]
++++
V_q^{2} - 2E\,V_q + \gamma\frac{\mu_p}{\mu_n}F^{2} = 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
V_q = E \pm \sqrt{E^{2} - \gamma\frac{\mu_p}{\mu_n}F^{2}},
++++

of which the positive root exceeds stem:[V_{DD} - V_{tn}] and is
inconsistent with the assumed triode pass-gate, leaving

[stem]
++++
\boxed{
V_q = (V_{DD} - V_{tn})
- \sqrt{(V_{DD} - V_{tn})^{2}
- \gamma\frac{\mu_p}{\mu_n}\bigl(V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|\bigr)^{2}}
}.
++++

This assumes ideal long-channel square-law behavior, a saturated
pull-up (stem:[V_q < |V_{tp}|]), an ideal stem:[0\,\mathrm{V}]
bitline, and no help from PG2 pulling stem:[\overline{Q}] up.

==== Write-stability condition and pull-up-ratio selection

The written node stem:[Q] drives INV2, whose switching point
stem:[V_m] is the one derived in <<bitcell-switching,Inverter Switching
Voltage>>, with stem:[B = \sqrt{\gamma\mu_p/(\beta\mu_n)}]. Write
stability requires stem:[V_q < V_m]:

[stem]
++++
(V_{DD} - V_{tn})
- \sqrt{(V_{DD} - V_{tn})^{2}
- \gamma\frac{\mu_p}{\mu_n}\bigl(V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|\bigr)^{2}}
\;<\;
\frac{V_{tn} + \sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_p\gamma}{\mu_n\beta}}
\bigl(V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|\bigr)}
{1 + \sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_p\gamma}{\mu_n\beta}}}.
++++

The boxed stem:[V_q] expression holds only while the pull-up is
saturated, stem:[V_q < |V_{tp}|]. With the values used in the hand
analysis stem:[V_m] is about stem:[0.37\,\mathrm{V}], above
stem:[|V_{tp}| = 0.3\,\mathrm{V}], so the inequality cannot be solved
for stem:[\gamma] with the boxed expression alone. Requiring the
stricter stem:[V_q < V_{tn}] keeps the boxed expression valid when
stem:[V_{tn} \le |V_{tp}|], guarantees that the NMOS of INV2 is off,
and gives the required pull-up ratio in closed form:

[stem]
++++
\boxed{
\gamma < \frac{\mu_n}{\mu_p}\cdot
\frac{V_{tn}\bigl(2V_{DD} - 3V_{tn}\bigr)}
{\bigl(V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|\bigr)^{2}}
}.
++++

==== Numerical evaluation — hand-analysis placeholders only

CAUTION: the numbers below are the LaTeX hand-analysis placeholders
at stem:[V_{DD} = 0.8\,\mathrm{V}], with
stem:[V_{tn} = |V_{tp}| = 0.3\,\mathrm{V}] and
stem:[\mu_n/\mu_p = 2]. They are _not_ GPDK 18 nm / post-layout results
at the nominal stem:[V_{DD} = 1.2\,\mathrm{V}], and no bench has run yet.

The closed-form bound gives stem:[\gamma < 1.68]. Evaluating the full
inequality numerically at stem:[\beta = 2], with the triode pull-up
current beyond stem:[V_q = |V_{tp}|], relaxes the limit to about
stem:[\gamma < 1.9]. For stem:[\gamma = 1] the divider puts stem:[V_q]
at stem:[0.27\,\mathrm{V}] and the current balance at
stem:[0.15\,\mathrm{V}] (0.146 V in the table); the divider is
pessimistic because a saturated pull-up supplies less current than a
resistor would.

.Written-node voltage against pull-up ratio at V_DD = 0.8 V, μn/μp = 2
[cols="1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| stem:[\gamma] | stem:[V_q] (V) | Margin to stem:[V_{tn}] (V) | Margin to stem:[V_m] at stem:[\beta = 2] (V)

| 0.5 | 0.067 | 0.233 | 0.285
| 1 | 0.146 | 0.154 | 0.220
| 1.5 | 0.250 | 0.050 | 0.126
| 2 | 0.40, pull-up in triode | fails | fails
|===

The bound scales directly with the NMOS-to-PMOS strength ratio. FinFET
kits often have a PMOS much closer to the NMOS than the planar
assumption of 2, and that decides whether a one-fin pass-gate can
overpower a one-fin pull-up:

.Sensitivity of the write bound to the NMOS-to-PMOS strength ratio
[cols="1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Strength ratio stem:[\mu_n/\mu_p] | stem:[\gamma] limit | stem:[V_q] at stem:[\gamma = 1] (V) | stem:[V_q] at stem:[\gamma = 0.5] (V)

| 2 | 1.68 | 0.146 | 0.067
| 1.5 | 1.26 | 0.211 | 0.092
| 1.25 | 1.05 | 0.276 | 0.113
| 1 | 0.84 | fails | 0.146
|===

Reducing stem:[\gamma] weakens the pull-up relative to the pass-gate
and lowers the written node, but stem:[\gamma] moves in whole-fin
steps, a stronger pass-gate lowers stem:[\beta] and loads the bitline,
and stem:[\gamma] also moves stem:[V_m]: the final pull-up ratio is not
selected by minimizing stem:[V_q] alone.
