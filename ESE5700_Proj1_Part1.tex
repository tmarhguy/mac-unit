\section*{Executive summary}
The symmetric 6T cell was sized by hand analysis and a three-point schematic $\beta$ sweep: at $0.8$ V, raising $\beta$ from 1 to 2 increases read SNM from $126.6$ to $156.3$ mV and lowers the read-disturbed zero node from $134.3$ to $77.1$ mV, so the $\beta=2$, $\gamma=1$ cell (PD 4 fins, PU 2 fins, PG 2 fins, $L=18$ nm) was selected. The cell was laid out with a horizontal wordline and a vertical bitline pair so that mirrored neighbours share bitline contacts and supply rails, and signed off with the course Pegasus DRC and LVS decks (Table~\ref{tab:area}: DRC \drcResult; LVS \lvsResult). \section{Assignment scope and evidence standard}
The current handout requires: sized symmetric schematic and hand analysis; a three-point schematic $\beta$ sweep; custom tileable layout with area and DRC/LVS summaries; per-cell BL and WL capacitance after extraction; post-layout hold/read SNM, write margin, cell current and supply dependence; a transient write/read sequence; and a discussion of the limiting trade-off. It also requires individual team contributions. Figures and numbers below are labeled as \emph{measured schematic}, \emph{hand estimate}, or \emph{open}. The supplied handout sets $F=10$ nm for area in $F^2$, and supply points of $0.8$, $0.7$ and $0.5$ V.

\section{Cell schematic and selected dimensions}
A 6T cell consists of cross-coupled inverters INV1 and INV2 and two pass-gates. Both storage nodes connect to precharged bitlines only while WL is high. For the candidate schematic, the captured device settings are $L=18$ nm and $n_{\rm fin}=4$ for each pull-down, $n_{\rm fin}=2$ for each pull-up, and $n_{\rm fin}=2$ for each pass-gate. Thus the nominal fin-count ratios are
\begin{equation}
\beta=\frac{(W/L)_{\rm PD}}{(W/L)_{\rm PG}}\simeq\frac{4}{2}=2,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{(W/L)_{\rm PU}}{(W/L)_{\rm PG}}\simeq\frac{2}{2}=1.
\label{eq:ratios}
\end{equation}
The effective process-model strengths are not simply fin counts; the ratios express the intended relative sizing. Both halves are identical, meeting the symmetric-cell requirement at schematic level.

\section{Hand sizing analysis}
\subsection{Read disturb and the cell ratio}
Assume $Q=0$, $\overline Q\approx V_{DD}$, WL$=V_{DD}$ and BL$=$BLB$=V_{DD}$. Current flows from BL through the pass-gate into $Q$ and down through the pull-down. The handwritten circuit and divider in Figure~\ref{fig:read-sketch} were part of the original analysis. They identify the two opposing devices and the node whose rise can upset INV2.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.88\linewidth,height=.29\textheight,keepaspectratio]{media/part1/6t-cell-wl-bl-inv12-q-qb.png}
\caption{Original handwritten read-state diagram, with node labels.}\label{fig:read-sketch}
\end{figure}
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.88\linewidth,height=.29\textheight,keepaspectratio]{media/part1/voltage_div_diagram.png}
\caption{Original handwritten read-divider sketch.}\label{fig:read-divider-sketch}
\end{figure}
For equal nominal channel length, a resistive first estimate gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{V_Q}{V_{DD}}=\frac{R_{\rm PD}}{R_{\rm PG}+R_{\rm PD}}
\approx\frac{1}{1+\beta},\qquad
V_Q\approx\frac{V_{DD}}{1+\beta}.
\label{eq:read-divider}
\end{equation}
At $0.8$ V, this simple expression predicts $0.400$, $0.320$ and $0.267$ V for $\beta=1$, $1.5$ and $2$. These are \emph{hand estimates}, much higher than the DC crossings in Table~1 because the devices are nonlinear and the ratio is only a proxy for their actual strengths. Their useful prediction is the downward trend with increasing $\beta$.

For a more physical but still ideal long-channel model, set $E=V_{DD}-V_{Tn}$ and $k=\mu_n C_{ox}(W/L)$. The pass-gate is saturated with $V_{GS}=V_{DS}=V_{DD}-V_Q$; the pull-down is in triode if $V_Q<E$. Neglecting leakage and body effect, Kirchhoff's law at $Q$ gives
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\tfrac12 k_{\rm PG}(E-V_Q)^2}_{I_{\rm PG}}
=\underbrace{k_{\rm PD}(EV_Q-\tfrac12 V_Q^2)}_{I_{\rm PD}}.
\label{eq:read-balance}
\end{equation}
Solving the physical root using $k_{\rm PD}/k_{\rm PG}\approx\beta$ yields
\begin{equation}
V_Q=E\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}\right).
\label{eq:read-root}
\end{equation}
The positive quadratic root violates the assumed operating region. The original handwritten steps in Figure~\ref{fig:read-notes} show the pass-gate current, current-ratio equation and closed form; the equations above state them legibly. Neither model substitutes for the GPDK simulation.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.95\linewidth,height=.22\textheight,keepaspectratio]{media/part1/ip_kpg_cox_vgs_vth_w_l_eq.png}
\caption{Original handwritten pass-gate current equation.}\label{fig:read-notes}
\end{figure}
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.95\linewidth,height=.22\textheight,keepaspectratio]{media/part1/kpd_kpg_e_vq_evq_eq.png}
\caption{Original handwritten PD/PG current balance.}
\end{figure}
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.95\linewidth,height=.22\textheight,keepaspectratio]{media/part1/vq_vdd_vth_beta_eq.png}
\caption{Original handwritten read-disturb voltage root.}
\end{figure}
The disturbed node drives INV2. The read criterion is $V_Q<V_M$, where $V_M$ is INV2's switching voltage. In a square-law inverter approximation,
\begin{equation}
V_M=\frac{V_{Tn}+B(V_{DD}-|V_{Tp}|)}{1+B},\quad
B=\sqrt{\frac{k_{\rm PU}}{k_{\rm PD}}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{\gamma\mu_p}{\beta\mu_n}}.
\label{eq:vm}
\end{equation}
$V_M$ is not independently measured here. Increasing $\beta$ lowers the disturb voltage, but it also changes $V_M$, area and later bitline loading; measured read SNM is therefore the deciding schematic quantity.
\FloatBarrier

\subsection{Write contention and the pull-up ratio}
For a write of zero into a stored one, take $Q\approx V_{DD}$ initially, $\overline Q\approx0$, BL$=0$, WL$=V_{DD}$. The pass-gate discharges $Q$ against an ON pull-up. The first-order divider reverses the read case:
\begin{equation}
\frac{V_Q}{V_{DD}}\approx\frac{R_{\rm PG}}{R_{\rm PG}+R_{\rm PU}}
\approx\frac{\mu_p\gamma}{\mu_n+\mu_p\gamma}.
\label{eq:write-divider}
\end{equation}
A smaller $\gamma$ weakens the pull-up relative to the pass-gate and lowers the written node. The receiving inverter must switch before the cell can settle back, so the condition is $V_Q<V_M$. If $\rho=\mu_n/\mu_p$, the divider implies $\gamma<\rho V_M/(V_{DD}-V_M)$, conditional on the assumed resistor model. Without a measured $V_M$ or process strengths this is a design inequality, not a numeric proof that $\gamma=1$ writes.

The more detailed hand current balance treats the pass-gate as triode and the pull-up as saturated while $V_Q<|V_{Tp}|$. With $E=V_{DD}-V_{Tn}$ and $F=V_{DD}-|V_{Tp}|$,
\begin{align}
I_{\rm PG}&=k_{\rm PG}(EV_Q-V_Q^2/2),\\
I_{\rm PU}&=\tfrac12 k_{\rm PU}F^2,\qquad
\frac{k_{\rm PU}}{k_{\rm PG}}\approx\gamma\frac{\mu_p}{\mu_n},\\
V_Q&=E-\sqrt{E^2-\gamma(\mu_p/\mu_n)F^2}.
\label{eq:write-root}
\end{align}
The other quadratic root is inconsistent with the assumed pass-gate region. Equation~\eqref{eq:write-root} also ceases to apply if the pull-up enters triode; no extracted model parameters have been inserted to make a fabricated numeric write limit. A stronger pass-gate helps writing but reduces $\beta$ and adds load; the $\gamma=1$ candidate needs a write-margin sweep before acceptance.

\subsection{From ViVA export to a margin}
Each run has a four-column \sourcepath{raw.csv} ViVA export. \sourcepath{scripts/check_snm_sweep.py} detects which trace is normal and which is reflected, writes normalized \sourcepath{q.csv} and \sourcepath{qb.csv}, mirrors one VTC about $y=x$, locates the three intersections, and fits the largest axis-aligned square within each of the two lobes. The reported margin is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNM}_{\rm read}=\min(\mathrm{SNM}_{L},\mathrm{SNM}_{R}).
\end{equation}
A 16,001-point voltage grid is used for the final result. Repeating the fit at 4,001 points changes the side length by at most $0.05$ mV. The square coordinates and crossings are retained in each \sourcepath{snm.json}. The low-voltage crossing is reported as the DC ``0''-node read-disturb operating point. This is a \emph{schematic} analysis, even though Part 1 ultimately asks for the same measurements after extraction.

\section{Layout (handout 1.2)}
\subsection{Floorplan and tiling intent}
The cell is laid out in \sourcepath{proj1_lib/6t-sram_final_layout/layout} (technology \texttt{cds\_ff\_mpt}, Virtuoso IC23.1 Layout XL) against the connectivity reference \sourcepath{proj1_lib/6t-sram/schematic}. The six PDK FinFET P-cells are kept at their schematic settings: $L=18$ nm, one finger, pull-ups PM0/PM1 at 2 fins, pull-downs NM0/NM1 at 4 fins, pass-gates NM3/NM2 at 2 fins. The floorplan follows the handout's tiling rule:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item the two pull-ups share one n-well strip under the top VDD rail; the pull-downs and pass-gates sit in the p-substrate region below it, so a vertically mirrored neighbour shares the rail and the well edge;
\item the wordline runs \emph{horizontally} across the full cell width and polysilicon gates of both pass-gates are tapped from it, so horizontally abutted cells form one continuous WL;
\item BL and BLB run \emph{vertically} on the metal level above WL at the left and right cell edges, over the pass-gate drain contacts, so a vertically mirrored neighbour shares each bitline contact;
\item VSS is placed on the bottom edge and VDD on the top edge so that both rails are shared by mirrored rows; $Q$ and $\overline Q$ stay internal.
\end{itemize}
Placement was generated from the schematic (Layout XL \emph{Generate All From Source}, then \emph{Place As In Schematic}) and the devices were then snapped to the PDK fin grid by hand; the signal nets were routed with the Virtuoso Space-based Router (\emph{Route $\to$ Automatic Routing}) restricted to the local-interconnect and M1/M2 layers, and any router result that violated the restricted-direction LiAct or M1 end-of-line rules was redrawn manually. Verification used the course Pegasus 23.2 DRC and LVS decks installed under \sourcepath{proj1_lib/lvs}, not the preliminary \emph{Verify $\to$ Process Rules} check.

\subsection{Density factor for the area accounting}
The handout estimates un-laid-out blocks as $A_{\rm est}=D\sum_i W_iL_i$ with $D=A_{\rm cell}/\sum_{\rm cell}W_iL_i$ measured from this cell. For FinFETs the drawn width is quantized, so we state the convention: one fin counts as the PDK fin pitch, $W_{\rm fin}=48$ nm (the \texttt{FinArea} grid of \texttt{cds\_ff\_mpt}), and $L=18$ nm for every device. The cell has $4+4+2+2+2+2=16$ fins, so
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\rm cell}W_iL_i=16\times0.048\times0.018\;\mu\mathrm m^2=0.0138\;\mu\mathrm m^2,
\qquad
D=\frac{A_{\rm cell}}{0.0138\;\mu\mathrm m^2}=\densityD .
\label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
The same $W_{\rm fin}$ convention must be used for every $\sum W_iL_i$ in Parts 2 and 3 so that $D$ cancels consistently.

\section{What limits the cell, and what changes for Part 2}
Read stability is set by the pass-gate/pull-down divider: the schematic sweep moved the disturbed zero node from $134$ mV ($\beta=1$) to $77$ mV ($\beta=2$) and raised read SNM by $30$ mV, and the extracted value in Table~\ref{tab:stability} is the one that must clear the Part 2 noise budget. The cost of $\beta=2$ is the four-fin pull-down, which sets the cell height and most of $C_{WL}$, and the two-fin pass-gate, which sets $C_{BL}$ and the read current that the Part 2 sense path has to integrate. Writability depends on $\gamma=1$ and is only proven by the write-margin sweep; if $\mathrm{WM}_{BL}$ at $0.5$ V is marginal, the remedy for Part 2 is a weaker pull-up or a write-assist (negative-BL or WL boost) in the column circuits rather than a cell change, so that the Part 1 characterization stays valid. For Part 2 we will keep this cell, mirror it to share BL contacts and the VDD/VSS rails, and size the wordline driver and precharge from $16\times C_{BL}$ and $4\times C_{WL}$ plus wire.
